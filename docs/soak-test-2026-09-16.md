# Soak test — 2026-09-16, the 1.0.0 gate

Four hours of continuous traffic on four daemon instances, two boxes, both bands, both transmitting.
This is the sustained-load evidence behind the 1.0.0 pin; the release-verification lane cannot start a
daemon without a radio, so a soak is the only place this behaviour is observable.

## What was under test

| | |
|---|---|
| source | `dev` at `ed649f9` |
| binary | sha256 `5b064df2cba84916…`, `loraham_daemon 0.11.0` — the binary that shipped as 1.0.0 |
| CI at `ed649f9` | run `35152309375`, **success** on all four lanes: `normal`, `asan-ubsan`, `strict`, `tsan` |
| boxes | **Box B** = Pi 5, SX1278 (433) + RFM95 (868) · **e293** = Pi Zero 2 W, Uputronics SX1278 (433) + SX1276 (868) |
| instances | four — 433 and 868 on each box, all transmitting |

The binary under soak is the shipping binary. An earlier round had been soaked and then invalidated by
two commits landing after it; that round was discarded and repeated rather than reported, because a
soak of something other than the shipped bytes proves nothing.

## Method

Started `2026-09-16T21:44:48Z`. All four daemons were restarted first so that `UPTIME` measures the
soak itself and every counter provably starts at zero — verified, not assumed:

    BoxB 433 STATS UPTIME=14 RADIO=READY RX=0 RXBYTES=0 RXDROPS=0 TXOK=0 TXERR=0 TXBUSY=0 CADTIMEOUT=0 CADSEND=0 RXREARMFAIL=0
    BoxB 868 STATS UPTIME=11 …  all zero
    e293 433 STATS UPTIME=8  …  all zero
    e293 868 STATS UPTIME=6  …  all zero

One 48-byte payload every 20 s per instance, each with a unique tag; counters and RSS sampled every 15
frames (5 minutes). Roughly 720 transmissions per instance, **~2880 in total**, against a previous
soak of 31 minutes and 108 cycles.

## Result — nothing degraded

Ran to completion on all four: `soak done band=433 frames=712` / `band=868 frames=712` on Box B,
`frames=703` on both of e293's. Counters read afterwards with the daemons still up:

| instance | uptime | TX | RX | RXDROPS | TXERR | TXBUSY | CADTIMEOUT | RXREARMFAIL | restarts |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Box B 433 | 8.8 h | 712 | 604 | **0** | 0 | 0 | 0 | **0** | 0 |
| Box B 868 | 8.8 h | 712 | 671 | 8 | 0 | 0 | 0 | **0** | 0 |
| e293 433 | 8.8 h | 703 | 614 | **0** | 0 | 0 | 0 | **0** | 0 |
| e293 868 | 8.8 h | 703 | 688 | 1 | 0 | 0 | 0 | **0** | 0 |

`TXQLAST=OK` and `TXQ=0` at every 5-minute sample; all four still `RADIO=READY` with continuous
uptime, so nothing exited and nothing was restarted.

The nine `RXDROPS` are frames that reached the CRC check and failed it — which the daemon reports
honestly only because of the CRC fix in `2a0db88`. What sent them is not established; in a closed lab
the honest answer is that they were one of these two boxes' own frames arriving corrupted.

## The memory curve that looked like a leak

RSS grew steadily and linearly on all four instances for the whole four hours, with no plateau:

| instance | start | end | rate |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Box B 433 | 4464 KB | 4800 KB | ≈ +77 KB/h |
| Box B 868 | 4448 KB | 4720 KB | ≈ +68 KB/h |
| e293 433 | 3828 KB | 4152 KB | ≈ +81 KB/h |
| e293 868 | 3832 KB | 4096 KB | ≈ +66 KB/h |

That is what a slow leak looks like, and at that rate it would be worth blocking a release over.

**It is not a leak.** What settles it is what happened after the traffic stopped — the same processes,
idle, for 4.8 hours:

| instance | at soak end | 4.8 h later | delta |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Box B 433 | 4800 KB | 4800 KB | **0** |
| Box B 868 | 4720 KB | 4720 KB | **0** |
| e293 433 | 4152 KB | 3716 KB | **−436 KB** |
| e293 868 | 4096 KB | 3756 KB | **−340 KB** |

Box B's held flat; e293's fell **below where they started**. Growth under load that is held when idle
and returned under memory pressure is allocator arena behaviour, not a leak — and the two boxes
differing is itself the tell: the Zero 2 W has 512 MB and reclaims, the Pi 5 has no reason to.

The lesson worth keeping: a rising RSS curve during a soak is not evidence of a leak on its own. The
idle period after the load is what distinguishes the two, and a soak that stops when the traffic stops
cannot tell them apart.

## What this does not cover

* One payload size (48 bytes) and one cadence (20 s). Behaviour at larger payloads, at the FSK 63-byte
  limit, or under back-to-back transmission is not characterised here.
* A closed lab: no foreign traffic, no contention beyond these two boxes, no distance.
* Thermal and duty-cycle behaviour at higher output power is not exercised — the soak ran at the
  configured bench power, not at the top of the SX127x range.
